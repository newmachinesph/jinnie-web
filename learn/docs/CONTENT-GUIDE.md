# Content guide

The binding contract is `content/schema.md`. This guide is the craft on top of it.

## Voice

Jinnie is formal but plain. Short declarative sentences. No exclamation marks, no slang, no emoji. She never shames anyone about debt or spending; she reports. Wrong answers get "Here is the number." She speaks in the first person only in Hook and Keep, and rarely: "I will remind you on payday."

Say what the product does, not what the learner should feel. "Net pay is the number your budget starts from" rather than "You'll feel so much more confident."

## Taglish

`tl` is Taglish as spoken in Metro Manila and on radio: a Filipino sentence frame with English money terms kept. "Ang net pay mo ay ang natitira pagkatapos ng deductions." Not deep Tagalog, not pure English, no forced hype. For the kids path, shorter and simpler. For the retired path, lean more Filipino, as an older reader speaks, but keep pension, account, OTP in English.

## Writing each beat

- **Hook**: ten seconds. A situation with a number in it, in the second person or with a named neighbour. No definitions.
- **Show**: the idea in two or three sentences, then two to four points at most. One number per point. The illustration carries the concept; the text names it.
- **Do**: the prompt is a question a person could answer at a kitchen table. `why` teaches the number or the reason, in one to three sentences. Never "Correct" or "Wrong" as the whole feedback.
- **Keep**: one line, memorable, true. Then an action that exists in Jinnie: a wish, a commitment, a deep link, a drill.

## Numbers

Use the facts sheet. Round illustrative amounts and say "illustrative" in `why` when the number is not official. The reference household is a ₱20,000 Metro Manila salary for First money, ₱30,000 to ₱60,000 for Building, ₱35,000 to ₱80,000 for Securing, ₱5,000 to ₱15,000 a month for Retired, baon scale for kids and teens. Anything not on the facts sheet goes to `VERIFY.md` with the source used.

## Interaction choices

- `predict` when the learner's intuition is likely wrong and the reveal will surprise.
- `number` when the arithmetic is the skill.
- `compare` when the trap is in the words of an offer.
- `spot` for fraud, always with realistic tells.
- `order` for procedures the learner will one day follow under stress.
- `story` to let a choice play out over months without a lecture.
- `swipe`, `rapid`, `match` for review and vocabulary; keep them short.

## Checklist before a lesson is called authored

- Five or six bites, no two consecutive with the same interaction type, one story, usually last.
- Every string has `en` and `tl`. Both read naturally aloud.
- Every `why` teaches something. Every Keep line stands alone.
- Amounts are on scale for the band. Facts come from the sheet or are flagged.
- `node --check content/<path>.js` passes. Play the lesson once on a phone-width window.

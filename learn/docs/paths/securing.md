# Securing (40 to 59)
For people with fifteen to twenty working years left, children still in school, ageing parents, and an SSS pension that the math itself shows will not be enough. The path opens with the arithmetic behind that shortfall, then works through health, saving, family trade-offs, estate, debt, and fraud for this age band.

## Lesson 1: securing.math — Retirement math
Objectives: Compute an SSS pension from the highest-of-three formula (formula (a), 40% of AMSC, and the floor), given an AMSC and credited years. Explain why the ten-year floor still pays a real pension, and why every credited year past ten compounds for life. Size the peso gap between a projected pension and retirement needs, in both monthly and lifetime terms, and see what five years of delay costs in required monthly savings.

Bites:
- `securing.math.1` predict — Guess the average monthly SSS pension actually paid today.
- `securing.math.2` number — Compute formula (a) for AMSC ₱8,000, 25 credited years.
- `securing.math.3` choice — Identify which of the three formulas actually pays for that worker.
- `securing.math.4` match — Match AMSC, CYS, the floor, and formula (a) to their definitions; the ten-year floor case (AMSC ₱6,000) is worked in the show text.
- `securing.math.5` number — Compute the monthly gap between ₱15,000 of needs and a ₱4,300 pension, extended to the lifetime total in the worked answer.
- `securing.math.6` story — Two starting points five years apart, same monthly gap, different totals at 60.
